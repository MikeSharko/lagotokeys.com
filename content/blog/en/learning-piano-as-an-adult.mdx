Many of my adult students say the same thing at the first lesson: "I always wanted to play." If that's you, here's the good news — it is not too late. **Piano lessons for adult beginners** work differently from lessons for children, and in some ways adults move faster: you understand theory quickly, you know what music you love, and you practice because you want to.

## A realistic first year at the piano

- **Months 1–3:** posture, hand position, reading notes in both clefs and your first simple pieces with both hands.
- **Months 4–6:** scales and chords, playing with the pedal, and a favorite melody arranged at your level.
- **Months 7–12:** longer pieces, a little sight-reading each week, and choosing the style you want to go deeper into — classical, pop, film music or jazz standards.

Everyone moves at their own pace; the plan is a guide, not a race.

## Fitting practice into a busy week

Twenty minutes on most days is plenty. Keep a short list on the music stand of exactly what to work on, so you can sit down and start without thinking. The [15-minute practice routine](/blog/daily-practice-routine/) I give children works for adults too — just add a few minutes to each block.

<Tip>
Don't wait until you "have more time." Start with 30-minute lessons — it's a comfortable pace for the first months, and you can move to 60 minutes when you're ready.
</Tip>

Lessons are one-on-one, so nothing is aimed at a group or at children. We choose pieces together, and the plan follows your goals — playing for yourself, for your family, returning to the piano after years away, or simply relaxing after work. Lessons are available in English or Russian.
