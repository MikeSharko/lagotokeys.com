Many of my adult students say the same thing at the first lesson: "I always wanted to play." Adults learn differently from children, and in some ways faster: you understand theory quickly, you know what music you love, and you practice because you want to.

## A realistic first year

- **Months 1–3:** posture, hand position, reading notes in both clefs and your first simple pieces with both hands.
- **Months 4–6:** scales and chords, playing with the pedal, and a favorite melody arranged at your level.
- **Months 7–12:** longer pieces, a little sight-reading each week, and choosing the style you want to go deeper into — classical, pop, film music or jazz standards.

## Fitting practice into a busy week

Twenty minutes on most days is plenty. Keep a short list on the music stand of exactly what to work on, so you can sit down and start without thinking.

<Tip>
Don't wait until you "have more time." Start with 30-minute lessons — it's a comfortable pace for the first months, and you can move to 60 minutes when you're ready.
</Tip>

Your goals set the plan. Whether you want to play for yourself, for your family or simply to relax after work, the lessons are built around you.
