Parents who grew up in Russia, Ukraine or Eastern Europe often look for a **Russian piano teacher in San Diego** because they remember what their own lessons gave them. Parents who didn't grow up there sometimes ask me the opposite question: what does "the Russian piano school" actually mean, and is it right for my child?

I trained at the St. Petersburg Conservatory and have taught for 30 years. Here's the tradition in plain words.

## Sound comes first

From the very first lessons, students learn to listen to the sound they make — not just press the right keys. We work on a singing tone, on soft and loud, on how one note connects to the next. The name of this site, *legato*, means exactly that: playing smoothly, connected, like a voice.

## A natural, relaxed hand

Good technique starts with posture: the height of the bench, a free shoulder, a round, relaxed hand. We build this slowly from day one, because a tense hand limits everything that comes later. Scales and studies are part of every week, in small doses.

## Reading and understanding music

Students learn to read fluently and to understand what they play: the form of a piece, the harmony, the character. This is what lets a student learn new music quickly on their own — and enjoy it for life.

## Real repertoire from the start

The Russian tradition gives beginners wonderful music written especially for them — Tchaikovsky's *Album for the Young*, pieces by Kabalevsky, along with Bach and Burgmüller. See some favorites in [beginner pieces students love](/blog/beginner-pieces-students-love/).

<Tip>
Demanding doesn't mean strict. With young children the standard is high, but the steps are small and every lesson ends with something they're proud of.
</Tip>

## Performing

Playing for others is part of learning. Twice a year, my students perform at a student concert for family and friends — a real goal to prepare for.

## Lessons in English or Russian

Every lesson can be taught in English, in Russian, or in both — which many bilingual families in San Diego appreciate. Lessons take place in person at my home studio in Rancho Peñasquitos, near Poway, Mira Mesa and Carmel Mountain.
