Every student has weeks when the piano feels like a chore. That's normal — even for children who love music. Parents often ask me **how to motivate a child to practice piano** without turning every evening into an argument. These three simple habits help students keep going until the next breakthrough.

## 1. Small, clear goals

"Practice the piece" is too vague. "Play bars 5 to 8 without stopping" is a goal you can finish today. Each week I write two or three of these goals in the student's notebook, so the child — and the parent — knows exactly what "done" looks like.

## 2. A practice chart

A sticker or a checkmark for every day at the piano — it sounds simple, and it works for adults too. A full week earns a small reward agreed in advance at home. Pair the chart with the [15-minute practice routine](/blog/daily-practice-routine/) so each day has a clear start and finish.

## 3. An audience at home

Once a week, hold a mini concert: one or two pieces for the family after dinner. Grandparents on a video call count too. Playing for someone gives practice a real purpose.

<Tip>
If motivation drops for more than a couple of weeks, tell me. Often a new piece or a different style is all it takes to bring the spark back.
</Tip>

## Pick music they actually want to play

Motivation grows when the music is theirs. Alongside lesson pieces, every student has one song they chose themselves — see the [beginner pieces students love](/blog/beginner-pieces-students-love/).

## A bigger goal: student concerts

Our student concerts twice a year work the same way, just on a bigger stage: a date on the calendar, family and friends in the audience, and a piece to be proud of. For many children in my San Diego studio, the next concert is the best practice motivator of all.
