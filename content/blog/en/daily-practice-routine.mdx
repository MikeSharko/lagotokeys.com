The thing I hear most from parents is "we just can't find time to practice." Good news: you don't need an hour. A **15-minute piano practice routine** done every day does more than one long session on the weekend — and it's the routine I give every new student in my San Diego studio.

Set a timer on the piano and work through the four blocks in order. Kids love seeing the timer run out; adults love knowing exactly what to do.

## The 15-minute piano practice routine

<Routine>
  <Step min={2} title="Warm up" color="butter">Five-finger patterns, slowly, with relaxed wrists.</Step>
  <Step min={3} title="Scales or technique" color="sky">One scale or exercise from this week's lesson.</Step>
  <Step min={7} title="The new piece" color="sage">Only the tricky bars — hands separately first, then together.</Step>
  <Step min={3} title="Just for fun" color="rose">Finish with a piece you already love playing.</Step>
</Routine>

## Why short daily practice works

Learning an instrument is mostly about building habits in the hands. The fingers learn through repetition spread over many days, not through one big push. When a child practices a little every day, each lesson starts where the last one ended instead of going back to fix what was forgotten.

Fifteen minutes is also short enough that it rarely turns into a battle at home. It fits between homework and dinner, and it's easy to say yes to.

## How long should a child practice piano?

As a rough guide from my own students:

- **Ages 5–8, first year:** 10–15 minutes a day.
- **Ages 9–12:** 15–25 minutes, depending on the pieces.
- **Teens and adults:** 20–30 minutes; more before a concert or exam.

The routine stays the same — only the minutes in each block grow.

## Same time, same place

Practice works best as a habit, not a decision. Pick a fixed moment — right after school, or before dinner — and keep the piano lid open so starting takes zero effort.

<Tip>
Slow and correct beats fast and messy. If a passage keeps breaking, play it at half speed three times perfectly — then speed up.
</Tip>

## How parents can help

Sit nearby for the last few minutes and ask your child to play you the "just for fun" piece. A small audience is the best motivation there is — no corrections needed, just listen. For more ideas, read [three ways to stay motivated between lessons](/blog/stay-motivated-between-lessons/).

If your child is just starting out, every new student gets this routine written in their notebook at the [trial lesson](/#book) — along with the first piece to practice.
