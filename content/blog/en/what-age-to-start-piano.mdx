Parents often ask me for one magic number. There isn't one, but for most children private piano lessons start to work well somewhere **between five and seven years old**. In 30 years of teaching I've learned that the best age to start piano lessons depends less on the birthday and more on a few simple signs of readiness.

## Signs your child is ready for piano lessons

- They can sit and focus on one activity for about fifteen minutes.
- They know their numbers and the first letters of the alphabet — we use both to name fingers and notes.
- They can move fingers one at a time, for example showing you just the ring finger.
- They can tell left from right, or are close to it.
- Most important: they ask about the piano and enjoy pressing the keys.

If you recognize three or four of these, your child is very likely ready.

## Starting piano at 4, 5 or 6

Four-year-olds can sometimes do well in short, playful lessons, but many get more from a year of singing, rhythm games and a group music class first. At five and six, most children are ready for a weekly **30-minute piano lesson**: long enough to learn something new, short enough to stay fun.

## If your child is a little too young

That's completely fine. Sing together, clap simple rhythms, listen to music and let them explore the piano without rules. When the signs above appear, it's time to start.

<Tip>
The trial lesson is the best way to find out. In half an hour I can usually tell whether a child is ready now or would enjoy it more in a few months — and I'll tell you honestly.
</Tip>

## Is it ever too late?

There is no "too late." Children who start at nine or ten often move faster, because reading and concentration are already in place. Teens and adults start all the time — see [piano lessons for adult beginners](/blog/learning-piano-as-an-adult/).
