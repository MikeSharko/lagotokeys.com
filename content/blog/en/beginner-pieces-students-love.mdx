The right piece at the right moment keeps a beginner coming back to the piano. I always mix classical music with songs students know from home, movies and games.

## Classical favorites

- **Minuet in G** from the Notebook for Anna Magdalena Bach — elegant and very satisfying to play.
- **Pieces from Tchaikovsky's "Album for the Young"** — short, full of character, and loved by students of every age.
- **Easy pieces by Kabalevsky and Burgmüller** — written especially for young pianists, each one teaches something new.
- **Ode to Joy** in a simple arrangement — often one of the very first "real" melodies.

## Modern music

Simplified arrangements of film and game themes, pop songs and well-known melodies are a wonderful reward. When a student brings me a song they love, we find a version that fits their level.

<Tip>
Every student keeps one "just for fun" piece going alongside their lesson pieces. It's the piece they play when nobody asks them to — and that's exactly the point.
</Tip>

## Why the mix matters

Classical pieces build technique and reading. Favorite songs build the desire to sit down and play. Together they make steady progress feel like fun, not homework.
