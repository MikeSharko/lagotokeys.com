The right piece at the right moment keeps a beginner coming back to the piano. When parents ask me for **easy piano pieces for beginners**, I always suggest a mix: classical music that builds real skills, and songs students already know from home, movies and games.

## Easy classical piano pieces for beginners

- **Ode to Joy** (Beethoven) in a simple arrangement — often one of the very first "real" melodies.
- **Minuet in G** from the Notebook for Anna Magdalena Bach — elegant and very satisfying to play.
- **Pieces from Tchaikovsky's "Album for the Young"** — short, full of character, and loved by students of every age.
- **Easy pieces by Kabalevsky** — written especially for young pianists; each one teaches something new.
- **Burgmüller's 25 Easy Studies** — the next step once reading is comfortable, with titles kids love, like "The Chase."

Many of these come from the Russian piano tradition I trained in — you can read more about [the Russian piano school](/blog/russian-piano-school/).

## Modern music for beginner piano students

Simplified arrangements of film and game themes, pop songs and well-known melodies are a wonderful reward. When a student brings me a song they love, we find a version that fits their level — so they practice it because they want to.

<Tip>
Every student keeps one "just for fun" piece going alongside their lesson pieces. It's the piece they play when nobody asks them to — and that's exactly the point.
</Tip>

## Why the mix matters

Classical pieces build technique and note reading. Favorite songs build the desire to sit down and play. Together they make steady progress feel like fun, not homework. And twice a year, students choose one of these pieces to perform at our student concert.
