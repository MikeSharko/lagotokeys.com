Searching for "piano lessons near me" in San Diego brings up music schools, franchises, marketplaces and private teachers. Choosing between them is hard if you don't play yourself. After 30 years of teaching, these are the seven questions I'd ask **before choosing a piano teacher** — whoever you choose.

## 1. What is the teacher's training?

A teacher with a music degree, especially in piano performance or pedagogy, can build solid technique from the start. Ask where they studied and how long they've taught.

## 2. Do they teach your child's age and level?

Teaching a five-year-old and teaching a teenager preparing for an exam are different skills. Ask who their students are today.

## 3. Is there a trial lesson?

A trial lesson lets your child meet the teacher before you commit. Afterwards you should get a clear plan — not just a schedule. Here's [what happens in a first piano lesson](/blog/first-piano-lesson/) at my studio.

## 4. In person or online?

For beginners, especially children, in-person lessons are worth the drive: the teacher can see and correct posture, hand shape and pedaling in a way video can't.

## 5. How long are the lessons?

Young children and first-year students usually do best with 30 minutes; progressing students, teens and adults with 60. A good teacher recommends a length after the trial.

## 6. Are there performance opportunities?

Recitals and student concerts give students a goal and a reason to practice. Ask how often they happen.

## 7. Is the location practical?

Weekly lessons add up. A studio 10–15 minutes away with easy parking makes the whole year easier.

<Tip>
Trust how your child feels after the trial. If they leave the lesson wanting to play again at home, that's the best sign you've found the right teacher.
</Tip>
