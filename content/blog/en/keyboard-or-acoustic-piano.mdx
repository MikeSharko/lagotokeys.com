You don't need a grand piano to start. But the instrument at home makes a big difference to how practice feels, so here is what I recommend to families.

## An acoustic piano

A real upright piano gives the richest sound and teaches the fingers the most. A good used upright is often affordable. Have it checked by a tuner before you buy, and plan on tuning once or twice a year.

## A digital piano

A digital piano is a great choice for many homes: it never needs tuning, takes less space and has a headphone jack for quiet practice. Look for these three things:

- **88 full-size keys**, so pieces never run out of keyboard.
- **Weighted, touch-sensitive keys** that feel heavier in the bass, like a real piano. Playing harder must sound louder.
- **A sustain pedal** that plugs in, ideally a real piano-style pedal rather than a small square switch.

## What to avoid

Small keyboards with 61 light keys and lots of rhythm buttons are fun toys, but they build weak fingers and habits that we later have to undo.
